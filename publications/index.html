Human-to-Robot Interaction: Learning from Video Demonstration for Robot Imitation

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Tuan Thanh Tran, Xiem HoangVan, Nak Young Chong

PaperarXiv ↗Code ↗Project
Abstract

Robotic manipulation through learning from demonstration aims to enable robots to acquire various actions efficiently. Unlike traditional learning from observation-action pairs, which is time-consuming and requires proficiency in coding. Our approach is inspired by the way humans learn and imitate actions in a more flexible and efficient manner by simply "watching" and "repeating" the actions. Inspired by this, we propose a "Human-to-Robot" imitation learning pipeline, which enables robots to learn diverse actions from video demonstrations. Specifically, we explicitly represent each step as two key tasks: (1) understanding the demonstration video and (2) learning the demonstrated manipulations. First, the video description is achieved by focusing on two essential commonsense properties: action understanding and object understanding. We utilize deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Large Vision Models (VLMs) to extract meaningful features related to these properties. Then, for execution, we develop a robot manipulation system built upon a Language Vision-conditioned architectures and Deep Reinforcement Learning. Experiments with real-world video demonstrations. The results show the robustness and adaptability of the two frameworks in various scenarios using a real robotic arm UR5.

Con-DSO: Learning Short-Horizon Consistency Priors for RGB-D Direct Sparse Odometry

Haolan Zhang, Thanh Nguyen Canh, C. Li, Z. Gao, X. Jiang, Nak Young Chong

PaperarXiv ↗CodeProject ↗
Abstract

RGB-D direct visual odometry (VO) benefits from metric depth measurements but often degrades in the presence of dynamic objects, occlusions, illumination changes, and unreliable depth, which violate the photometric and geometric consistency assumptions of direct alignment. We propose Con-DSO, a consistency-aware RGB-D direct sparse odometry framework that addresses these challenges through a unified learned uncertainty model. A dual-branch consistency network is trained on adjacent RGB-D frame pairs using flow-guided photometric errors and projective depth-consistency errors to predict pixel-level photometric and geometric uncertainty. The predicted uncertainty is first converted into pairwise quality to guide support-pixel selection and is then fused across adjacent frame pairs to form a host-side quality prior for keyframe-based tracking. To account for the different roles of photometric and depth information in direct RGB-D optimization, the quality prior is incorporated through a decoupled photometric-geometric weighting scheme, with the geometric weight applied only to the translational component of pose estimation. Experiments on five public RGB-D benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over direct RGB-D odometry baselines, achieving more than 20\% reduction in absolute trajectory error on ICL-NUIM and approximately 50\% to 80\% reductions on RGB-D Scenes V2, TUM/BONN, and OpenLORIS. These results demonstrate that learned consistency-aware uncertainty can substantially improve the robustness of RGB-D direct visual odometry in challenging environments.

Monocular vSLAM for Colonoscopy: Recent Advances, Benchmarking, and Future Perspectives

Duc Manh Do, Van Bang Nguyen, Thanh Nguyen Canh, Mai Thanh Thai, Xiem HoangVan

PaperarXivCodeProject ↗
Abstract

Colonoscopy is the gold standard for colorectal cancer screening, yet its limited field of view constrains spatial awareness, mucosal coverage assessment, and reliable lesion localization. Monocular visual simultaneous localization and mapping (vSLAM) has emerged as a promising approach for reconstructing three-dimensional (3D) colonic anatomy and estimating endoscope motion from standard colonoscopic video without additional sensing hardware. However, the colonoscopic environment poses substantial challenges, including weak and repetitive texture, specular reflections, illumination variability, and tissue deformation. This survey provides a comprehensive review of monocular vSLAM for colonoscopy, tracing its evolution from classical feature-based and direct methods to learning-based approaches, neural scene representations, and deformation-aware formulations. We summarize representative systems, publicly available datasets, evaluation protocols, and emerging clinical applications, while critically analyzing how existing methods address the unique constraints of colonoscopic imaging. Furthermore, we present a unified benchmarking study of representative monocular vSLAM approaches on the C3VDv1 dataset, enabling direct comparison of localization accuracy and reconstruction quality under a standardized evaluation framework. Finally, we discuss key open challenges, including dataset realism, standardized benchmarking, uncertainty-aware depth estimation, non-rigid reconstruction, semantic integration, and clinical validation, and outline future research directions toward robust, accurate, and clinically deployable monocular vSLAM systems for colonoscopy.

Collaborative SLAM in the Era of 3D Gaussian Splatting: Architectures, Methods, and Future Directions

Phuc Nguyen Xuan, Thanh Nguyen Canh, Huu-Hung Nguyen, Nak Young Chong, Xiem HoangVan

PaperarXiv ↗CodeProject ↗
Abstract

Multi-robot Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) demands scene representations that are simultaneously real-time, high-fidelity, and fusable across agents. Neither sparse feature maps nor neural implicit fields fully satisfy these three requirements. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) renders explicit Gaussian primitives through differentiable rasterization, offering both speed and photorealism. However, its dense, continuously optimized submaps strain communication budgets and require radiometric and geometric consistency across agents. This survey reviews Collaborative Gaussian Splatting SLAM (CoGS-SLAM), an emerging class of systems that build a shared Gaussian world model across robot teams. We propose an architecture-based taxonomy that contrasts centralized designs, which still dominate due to the computational and synchronization demands of 3DGS, with nascent distributed alternatives. We then provide a problem-oriented analysis of four core challenges: inter-agent correspondence and association, multi-agent registration and alignment, multi-agent consistency maintenance, and communication-efficient collaboration. Beyond individual systems, we identify cross-cutting design patterns, ranging from shared semantic representations to hierarchical scene organization and asynchronous fusion. We further review benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics, and compare representative systems in terms of tracking accuracy, rendering and map-fusion quality, and communication cost and scalability. This comparison highlights how submap-level optimization and consensus-based pose estimation underpin recent performance gains. Finally, we outline open challenges, including information-aware Gaussian sharing beyond compression, lifelong collaborative mapping, semantic and instance-level understanding, multi-modal fusion across heterogeneous robot teams, and Sim2Real robustness. This survey offers newcomers a structured map of the field and provides system designers with concrete criteria for selecting architectures, representations, and communication strategies.

BPDA-GMM: Bayesian Probabilistic Data Association via Gaussian Mixture Models for Semantic SLAM

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Haolan Zhang, Antonio Sgorbissa, Xiem HoangVan, Nak Young Chong

Paper ↗arXiv ↗Code ↗Project ↗
Abstract

Probabilistic data association (PDA) improves semantic SLAM under perceptual aliasing. However, existing formulations birth new landmarks from hand-tuned null weights or fixed gates, and they either freeze association weights at observation time or recompute them as the map grows. Accumulated association evidence is therefore not carried forward. To address these limitations, we propose BPDA-GMM, an online Bayesian PDA framework for semantic SLAM with a growing object map. BPDA-GMM formulates association as a Chinese Restaurant Process (CRP)-weighted Gaussian mixture posterior over existing landmarks and a new-landmark hypothesis. The CRP prior accumulates association evidence in O(1) counts and calibrates landmark birth to the scene. A semantic-geometric likelihood gates candidates, and ambiguity-triggered tempering sharpens the posterior only when no dominant hypothesis exists. The soft posterior updates semantic Gaussian landmarks in the front-end, and mature dominant components become decoupled max-mixture factors in the back-end. Experiments in simulation and on real indoor and outdoor datasets show that BPDA-GMM reduces duplicate landmarks and improves trajectory accuracy, semantic map quality, and robustness to perceptual aliasing and classifier errors.

Semantic Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping: A survey on state of the art, challenges, and future directions

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Haolan Zhang, Xiem HoangVan, Nak Young Chong

Paper ↗arXiv ↗CodeProject ↗
Abstract

Semantic Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (Semantic vSLAM) is a critical area of research in robotics and computer vision, focusing on the simultaneous localization of robotic systems and the association of semantic information to construct the most accurate and comprehensive model of the surrounding environment. Since the first foundational work on Semantic vSLAM appeared more than two decades ago, the field has attracted increasing attention across various scientific communities. Despite its significance, the field lacks comprehensive surveys encompassing recent advances and persistent challenges. In response, this study provides a thorough examination of the state-of-the-art of Semantic vSLAM techniques, with the aim of illuminating current trends and key obstacles. Beginning with an in-depth exploration of the evolution of visual SLAM, this study outlines its strengths and unique characteristics while also critically assessing previous survey literature. Subsequently, a unified problem formulation and evaluation of the modular solution framework is proposed, which decomposes the problem into discrete stages, including visual localization, semantic feature extraction, mapping, data association, and loop closure optimization. Moreover, this study investigates alternative methodologies such as deep learning and the utilization of large language models, alongside a review of relevant research about contemporary SLAM datasets. Concluding with a discussion on potential future research directions, this study serves as a comprehensive resource for researchers seeking to navigate the complex landscape of Semantic vSLAM.

SR-SLAM: Scene reliability-based RGB-D SLAM in diverse environments

Haolan Zhang, Chenghao Li, Thanh Nguyen Canh, Lijun Wang, Nak Young Chong

Paper ↗arXiv ↗CodeProject ↗
Abstract

Visual simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) plays a critical role in autonomous robotic systems, especially where accurate and reliable measurements are essential for navigation and sensing. In feature-based SLAM, the quantity and quality of extracted features significantly influence system performance. Due to the variations in feature quantity and quality across diverse environments, current approaches face two major challenges: (1) limited adaptability in dynamic feature culling and pose estimation, and (2) insufficient environmental awareness in assessment and optimization strategies. To address these issues, we propose SR-SLAM, a scene reliability-based framework that enhances feature-based SLAM through environment-aware processing. Our method introduces a unified scene reliability assessment mechanism that incorporates multiple metrics and historical observations to guide system behavior. Based on this assessment, we develop: (i) adaptive dynamic region selection with flexible geometric constraints, (ii) depth-assisted self-adjusting clustering for efficient dynamic feature removal in high-dimensional settings, and (iii) reliability-aware pose refinement that dynamically integrates direct methods when features are insufficient. Furthermore, we propose (iv) reliability-based keyframe selection and a weighted optimization scheme to reduce computational overhead while improving estimation accuracy. Extensive experiments on public datasets and real-world scenarios show that SR-SLAM outperforms state-of-the-art dynamic SLAM methods, achieving up to 90% improvement in accuracy and robustness across diverse environments. These improvements directly contribute to enhanced measurement precision and reliability in autonomous robotic sensing systems.

Abstract

Keyframe visual-inertial SLAM weights every feature by a content-blind covariance, commits its initialization on a timer, and takes metric scale from excitation alone. UC-SLAM, built on ORB-SLAM3, tests a replacement for each under a registered protocol. Foundation-model metric depth enters through a log-depth factor with a per-keyframe log-scale chain designed to absorb the network's scene-dependent bias. USGLoc: Uncertainty-Aware Semantic Visual Localization from 3D Gaussian Splatting

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Xiem HoangVan, Nak Young Chong

PaperarXivCode ↗Project ↗
Abstract

A visual localizer should tell a robot where the camera is and how much to trust that answer, because the pose is fused into a Bayesian state estimator. Recent semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) localizers are accurate but return a single pose with no confidence, while uncertainty-aware mapping methods quantify map reliability but do not localize. We present USGLoc, an evidential localizer built on one principle: the map's own uncertainty drives every stage and survives to the output. Each primitive of an evidential semantic Gaussian map carries a Dempster-Shafer belief over open-vocabulary semantics with an explicit vacuity mass, and the query is encoded into the same space. Vacuity gates 2D-3D semantic matching, which turns retrieval ambiguity into a pose mixture; it gates the correspondences of a render-and-match correction; and it weights the rendering residual per pixel. A tempered Laplace approximation turns each refined hypothesis into a covariance, giving a Gaussian-mixture posterior on SE(3) that condenses to one Gaussian for a filter. Without an external pose prior, USGLoc localizes to 0.83 cm/0.26° on average on 7Scenes and 0.62 cm/0.26° on 12Scenes. The poses it trusts most are the most accurate in our comparison: 0.30 cm on its most-confident tenth of the 7Scenes queries, against 0.46 cm for a strong scene-coordinate baseline ranked by a PnP covariance. The tempered covariance is close to nominal at the 68% level and over-confident in the tails.

Uncertainty-Aware Open-Vocabulary Gaussian Splatting for Changing Scenes

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Haolan Zhang, Xiem HoangVan, Nak Young Chong

PaperarXivCode ↗Project
Abstract

A robot that reuses a semantic map must know how much evidence supports each part of it and when this evidence is out of date. We present a probabilistic open-vocabulary field for 3D Gaussian Splatting that addresses both. Each Gaussian holds a von Mises-Fisher posterior over the direction of its vision-language feature, and each keyframe counts as at most one observation. The sufficient statistics give two uncertainties: an epistemic one, the weighted number of views, and an aleatoric one, the agreement of the views, which does not shrink when evidence is scarce. A feature residual detects change, with a label-free threshold from the map's own mapping pass. A flagged Gaussian is reset and rejoins the robust map only once its observed depth matches its position. With few views, the epistemic uncertainty ranks the errors that more views fix (6/6 Replica test scenes); with many views, the aleatoric one ranks the errors that remain (6/6 Replica, 8/8 ScanNet). On ten real scenes in which an object was removed, the reset lowers the share of the object's former pixels that still answer its class from 34% (averaging) to 21%. The field matches averaging the same features, is better calibrated than trained maps, and builds its semantic layer two orders of magnitude faster. As a ranker of stale Gaussians, our field read over the whole update matches the strongest baseline (AP 64.6 vs 64.7) but trails it when both are fused with depth (81.0 vs 84.9).

Which Objects Stay? Learning Hierarchical Persistence Beliefs for Lifelong Open-Vocabulary Object Maps

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Haolan Zhang, Nak Young Chong

PaperarXiv ↗Code ↗Project ↗
Abstract

Lifelong object maps must estimate whether an object remains at its previously observed location after it leaves view. Existing persistence filters rely on class-based survival priors, making them difficult to apply reliably in open-vocabulary settings. We introduce HiPer, a hierarchical persistence filter that derives persistence beliefs directly from data rather than semantic categories. For a new object, HiPer retrieves visually and geometrically similar training instances and estimates an initial survival rate from their observed histories, without requiring class labels. This estimate initializes a conjugate Beta belief over the object's survival probability, which is updated in closed form using detections and missed observations under Rosen’s persistence-filter framework. When an object is re-identified at a new location, its persistence belief is transferred with the object, enabling the map to learn object-specific mobility over time. Experiments on 120 held-out simulated sequences show that HiPer outperforms all compared methods. Retrieval-based priors outperform CLIP-derived label priors, and object-specific online learning provides further gains. Results on 47 held-out 3RScan environments demonstrate that the proposed retrieval-based prior also outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in real-world settings.

Abstract

A robot that navigates from a semantic map inherits every silent error of the network that built it. Existing evidential mapping methods summarize this risk as a single classification-derived scalar, and they stop at a labeled map. A planner then cannot tell why a cell is unreliable, or how it may be used. We present HESFM. It separates a structural semantic map from a functional affordance layer, and threads one evidential signal between them, from pixel to plan. This echoes the neuroimaging distinction between structural connectivity (diffusion tensor imaging) and functional connectivity (fMRI). We decompose each cell's uncertainty into four physical sources (evidential, coherence, geometric, and temporal). Efficient Continuous Semantic Mapping based on Spatio-Temporal Awareness

My Le Pham, Dinh Trieu Duong, Xiem HoangVan, Thanh Nguyen Canh

PaperarXiv ↗CodeProject
Abstract

Continuous semantic mapping allows autonomous robots to understand both the spatial structure and the semantic content of complex environments. However, most existing methods process the entire space, treat voxels as independent units, and do not keep the semantic labels consistent over time. This leads to high computational cost and reduced robustness in dynamic scenes. This paper proposes a semantic mapping method that brings spatial and temporal relationships into the semantic inference process. The method adjusts the inference range according to the local semantic uncertainty and fuses labels over time to improve map stability and computational efficiency. Experiments on the SemanticKITTI dataset show that the proposed method improves mapping accuracy by about 12% and reaches an mIoU of 54.92%, which is 13.18 percentage points higher than spatial-only mapping. These results show that spatiotemporal reasoning is effective for continuous semantic mapping in autonomous robotic systems.

Decoupled Object-Centric Video Understanding for Generating Robotic Manipulation Commands

Thanh Nguyen Canh, Tuan Thanh Tran, Haolan Zhang, Z. Gao, Xiem HoangVan, Nak Young Chong

PaperarXiv ↗CodeProject
Abstract

Translating video demonstrations into executable robot commands remains challenging because existing methods often fail to identify which objects are functionally involved in the demonstrated action. As a result, they may generate commands that are linguistically plausible but operationally ambiguous. We propose an object-centric video understanding framework that decouples action recognition from object identification to generate precise, grammar-free manipulation commands. Our approach integrates Temporal Shift Modules (TSM) for efficient spatio-temporal action classification with a novel Object Selection algorithm that identifies task-relevant objects through trajectory-based role classification, blur detection, and overlap minimization. The selected objects are then processed by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robust category recognition and zero-shot generalization. Evaluated on a modified Something-Something V2 dataset, our method achieves 86.79% action classification accuracy and BLEU-4 scores of 0.337 on standard objects and 0.261 on novel objects. These results improve over the strongest task-specific baseline by 80.2% and 143.9%, respectively. Larger gains are observed in METEOR and CIDEr, reaching 157.9% and 171.7% on novel objects. Across all semantic metrics, our approach consistently outperforms task-specific methods and remains competitive with, or surpasses, large general-purpose VLMs while retaining a modular, object-centric design.

Abstract

Hybrid TD3: Overestimation Bias Analysis and Stable Policy Optimization for Hybrid Action Space

Tuan Thanh Tran, Thanh Nguyen Canh, Nak Young Chong, Xiem HoangVan

Paper ↗arXiv ↗Code ↗Project
Abstract

Reinforcement learning in discrete-continuous hybrid action spaces presents fundamental challenges for robotic manipulation, where high-level task decisions and low-level joint-space execution must be jointly optimized. Existing approaches either discretize continuous components or relax discrete choices into continuous approximations, which suffer from scalability limitations and training instability in high-dimensional action spaces and under domain randomization. In this paper, we propose Hybrid TD3, an extension of Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) that natively handles parameterized hybrid action spaces in a principled manner. We conduct a rigorous theoretical analysis of overestimation bias in hybrid action settings, deriving formal bounds under twin-critic architectures and establishing a complete bias ordering across five algorithmic variants under synchronized Gaussian error assumptions. Building on this analysis, we introduce a weighted clipped Q-learning target that marginalizes over the discrete action distribution, achieving equivalent bias reduction to standard clipped minimization while improving policy smoothness. Experimental results demonstrate that Hybrid TD3 achieves superior training stability and competitive performance against state-of-the-art hybrid action baselines.

Experimental results show that the proposed TWSAC in TW-TSC achieves higher performance than both fixed-time TSC methods and relevant RL algorithms.
